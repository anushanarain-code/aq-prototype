AIM AHIMSA QUOTIENT — CURRENT METHODOLOGY SOURCE
Source: AIM of India — Ethical Scoring-AQ
URL: https://aimofindia.org/ethical-scoring/

IMPORTANT SOURCE RULE
This file contains only the current three-band AQ framework.
Legacy numerical AQ scoring material is intentionally excluded.
Do not infer or generate a numerical AQ score.

1. PURPOSE

AIM uses the Ahimsa Quotient (AQ) framework to classify publicly listed
companies according to how much their business kills or harms animals.

The framework is intended to help distinguish companies whose business
activities cause relatively less or more harm to animals.

2. KEY TERMS

Himsak means harm-causing: an activity, ingredient or product that kills
or harms living beings.

Ahimsak means non-harming: an activity that avoids or reduces harm to
living beings.

3. THREE AQ BANDS

GREEN — HIGH AQ
Minimal or no involvement in animal harm.

ORANGE — MEDIUM AQ
A genuine but smaller animal-linked involvement, or finance activity
where money is spread across a wide range of industries.

RED — LOW AQ
Direct, significant, or core involvement in animal harm.

The colour band is a broad indication. It is not an exact numerical
measurement.

4. MAIN TEST

AIM rates a company on what its business actually does — its products
and the money it earns — rather than every possible indirect link.

A real and significant animal product or activity can move a company
to a worse band.

A very small, easily replaceable, or far-fetched animal link does not
by itself move a company to a worse band.

A company's own business mix matters; two companies in the same sector
can therefore receive different AQ bands.

5. CORE VS MINORITY TEST

Core or large share:
Animal product or activity is the heart of the business or generates
a large share of revenue → Red.

Real but smaller line:
Genuine animal-linked product or activity exists but is a minority
part of an otherwise animal-neutral business → Orange.

Tiny, replaceable or far-fetched:
The link is very small, easily replaced by a non-animal option, or only
a distant possibility → Green.

6. EXAMPLES OF THINGS THAT DO NOT BY THEMSELVES MOVE A COMPANY DOWN

Serving many industries is not by itself a basis for a worse AQ band.
Generic supply, services, software, logistics, transport, utilities,
rent or advertising sold to many industries do not count merely because
some customers may harm animals.

Environmental or habitat impact does not by itself determine the AQ
band. AQ measures harm to animals.

Distant or far-fetched links do not by themselves make a company Red.

7. SPECIAL RULES

Products specifically created for animal-rearing or animal-processing
industries can affect the AQ classification.

Products designed to kill insects or rodents are classified as Red.

Core pharmaceutical and cosmetic makers are classified as Red under
AIM's stated animal-testing rule. Contract research organisations and
biotech firms conducting animal testing or depending on animal-derived
lab materials are also classified as Red under the framework.

Hospitals that merely use medicines remain Green under the framework.

Synthetic substitutes for animal-derived materials are treated as
Green under the framework.

Animal-derived material built into a product can result in Red
classification.

Businesses that directly sell or serve animal food can move to a worse
band depending on the scale of that activity.

8. FINANCE COMPANIES

Finance companies are judged according to what their money ultimately
supports.

Finance companies whose money is spread across a wide mix of industries,
including animal-based businesses, are classified Orange.

Money restricted to one animal-neutral purpose, or pure market
infrastructure that does not itself direct capital into animal
businesses, can be classified Green.

9. CLASSIFICATION EVIDENCE

AIM uses publicly available information and company-published
disclosures.

Sources can include:
- annual reports
- investor presentations
- segment disclosures
- company websites
- product catalogues
- regulatory filings
- stock-exchange disclosures
- sustainability, ESG and integrated reports
- industry reports and market research
- credible media sources
- government agencies
- recognised industry associations

AIM states that it verifies underlying facts before relying on an
assumption.

Where evidence is limited or conflicting, AIM states that it temporarily
assigns the safer higher-harm band until better information becomes
available.

10. CLASSIFICATION PROCESS

AIM's stated process is:

1. Examine the company's actual business, products, raw materials,
   business segments and revenue mix.

2. Apply the main test: determine whether the company's own animal
   involvement is a real and significant part of the business or only
   a small or far-fetched link.

3. Check the share of relevant activity and apply the framework rules.

4. Assign Red, Orange or Green and document a factual explanation.

5. Peer-check classifications against similar companies and revisit
   classifications when new information becomes available.

11. LIMITATIONS

AIM states that ahimsa is a subjective idea and that no classification
can be fully objective.

Companies can operate across several business segments, making it
difficult to separate all animal-linked and animal-neutral revenue.

Ratings rely on publicly available information and judgement and may
change when additional information becomes available.

The AQ band is a broad indication rather than an exact measurement.

12. AI USE RULES

The AQ dataset supplied by the application is authoritative for the
specific company's current AQ band.

The AI must never independently classify a company.

The AI must never change, override or infer a company's AQ band.

The AI may explain a supplied AQ classification using:
- the company's supplied AIM justification;
- the company's supplied AQ band;
- the methodology in this source file.

The AI must not invent company activities, products, revenue,
animal links, ethical practices, sustainability claims, or external
sources.

If the supplied company information does not establish an answer, the
AI must say that the available AIM information does not establish the
answer.

The AI must not introduce numerical AQ scores.

The AI must not use general model knowledge to fill missing company
information.
